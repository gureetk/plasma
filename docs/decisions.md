## Milestone 1: live TV

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sample world or live | Until the first source is added, Plasma runs on the boards' sample world, with the clock at 20:41 on Wed 21 Oct. From then on it runs on the source's channels and the real time; deleting the last source brings the sample world back. Catch-up's sample programs, Recordings, Multiview and Who's Watching stay on sample data until their milestones; Search, Movies, Series and Home's Continue Watching and New in Movies have moved to the sources' data in M2 and M4, and Home's Apps row and Apps to the TV's own apps in M5, in the sample world too. | Every screen has something to show from the first start. |
| Wiring | Plain constructors in one `AppGraph`, built when the app starts, instead of a dependency-injection library. | Few enough parts that a library adds more than it saves. |
| Sources in M1 | Xtream Codes logins and M3U playlists by address. Stalker portals come with M3; Connect on one says so. | The owner's server is Xtream, and it also hands out its list as an M3U playlist. |
| Keeping a source | A new source whose channels don't load isn't kept, so Connect can simply be tried again, even when Add a Source is left while it connects. A refresh that fails, or that finds no live channels at all, keeps the channels and guide there were and notes the problem on the source. A guide refresh is written beside the old guide and shown only once it's complete, in one step. Imports run off the main thread. | A failed import never empties a list (brief 5.1). |
| A source's actions | OK on a source in Portals & Playlists offers Refresh Now and Delete Source; Delete asks first, on Cancel. Edit, Set as Primary, Test Connection, Account Info, Playback for This Source and Disable come with M3. | Enough to try a source and take it away again. |
| Secrets at rest | Xtream passwords, playlist addresses and guide addresses are sealed with a key the Android Keystore holds. Channel addresses from a playlist, which usually carry a login, are encrypted with a data key that the Keystore key seals in turn, because sealing thousands of them through the Keystore would take minutes. | Brief 6.10. |
| Playlist channels | Each keeps its place across refreshes by the stream id at the end of an Xtream-style address (`…/user/pass/1234.ts`), or else by its guide id and name, or else its name; a repeat gets "#2", "#3"… Only live entries are imported: addresses under `/movie/` or `/series/`, or ending in a video file's extension, stay out of Live TV. `#EXTVLCOPT` lines may set the user agent and referrer, nothing else. | Addresses often carry a token that changes (brief 7.3). |
| Playlist guides | The guide addresses typed in on Add a Source, or else the ones the playlist names (`url-tvg`, `x-tvg-url`). With several guides, a channel takes its programs from the first that has it. | |
| How streams play | Live MPEG-TS over http(s) goes through the stream hub; an address ending in `.m3u8`, and `udp://`, `rtmp://` and the like, go straight to VLC. When the hub finds an HLS or DASH playlist behind an address that didn't say so, or can't open an address at all, VLC opens it from the provider itself. A live stream that ends is reported as the source stopping, with Try Again. | Brief 5.4.2. |
| Leaving Plasma and the TV turning off | The picture and sound stop at once; the provider connection stays open for a minute, so coming straight back plays on from the live edge without reconnecting, and then it's let go. Right after a zap, the connection still open is the channel before's, so it goes at once. When the TV Turns Off: Stop at Once lets it go straight away, Keep Playing doesn't stop at all. A partial wake lock (70 s) keeps the minute running. On a box (no speakers of its own), the HDMI output going away counts as the TV turning off. | Brief 5.20. A TV's own Android sees its soundbar's HDMI come and go, so the HDMI hint is for boxes only. |
| When the TV turns on | Last Channel opens the player on the last channel watched (unless the player is already showing), Plasma Home goes to Home, TV Guide to the Guide. Coming back from another app carries on where Plasma was. Since M5 it applies when Plasma comes up within a minute of the TV coming on (time for a profile picker or the launcher first), and when the TV starts with Plasma as its Home or with the override on; Plasma opened later, from another app, carries on. | Brief 5.13. |
| Match Frame Rate | The full-screen player only, never Home's muted picture. 25 and 29.97 fps ask for 50 and 59.94, the rate deinterlaced broadcasts play at. Android 12 and up honor "seamless only"; Android 11 takes the rate as a hint; older TVs switch the display mode only with "always", since that blanks the screen. | Brief 5.4.4. |
| Audio passthrough | libVLC passes through each format the HDMI output reports and decodes the rest, so Passthrough and Auto behave alike with VLC; Decode to PCM turns passthrough off. Settings › Audio shows the output and its formats as this TV reports them. | Brief 5.4.6; per-format choice is VLC's own. |
| APKs | One APK per ABI (`armeabi-v7a`, `arm64-v8a`) plus a universal one with both. libVLC's x86 libraries stay out. With VLC alone, Plasma Dev is 48 MB for `armeabi-v7a`, 63 MB for `arm64-v8a` and 104 MB universal. | Brief 5.18 and 6.8, and the owner's setup; the sizes are the M1 measurement the brief asks for (6.7). |
| Locked channels | Opening a locked channel asks for the sample PIN (1234) until profiles and parental controls arrive (M7). Until it's unlocked, a locked channel shows no live picture in Live TV's and the Guide's previews or behind Home, and zapping onto it in the player shows nothing. | |
| Screen on while playing | The screen is kept on while live video opens or plays, so the TV's own screensaver doesn't stop Plasma and the channel with it. Plasma's own screensaver still comes after ten idle minutes away from the player. | Brief 5.14 and 5.20. |
| Still to come in the player | Pause and rewind act on live TV since M6's rewind buffer (see Milestone 6), and Start Over since M2. In Player Options, Switch Player works since M3, and so do Audio and Subtitles, Picture Size, the stream info and Sleep Timer (see Milestone 3). | |

## Milestone 2: the guide, catch-up, reminders and search

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Guide by day | Each day tab loads that day's programs from the database, with four hours either side of midnight, and shows empty cells while they load. The Group button (or yellow) picks All Channels, Favorites or one of the provider's groups; Favorites count from 1 here too. A channel number jumps to its row at the same time. | Brief 4.8. |
| An out-of-date guide | When no guide has been loaded for two days, or the programs run out within 12 hours, an amber line under the Guide's header says when it was last updated, with Refresh. | Brief 5.3. |
| Reminders | Kept in the database per program, so they survive a restart, and shown as bells in the Guide and on Live TV. A minute before the start, the toast from board 11 comes up with Switch Now focused and stays until it's acted on or the program ends. In the player it's part of the player, so channel and number keys still work under it; over any other screen it's a window of its own, so the screen keeps its focus and gets it back. Since M5 it can show over other apps too (see Milestone 5). | Brief 4.11 and 5.7. |
| When reminders fire | From Plasma's own clock while Plasma is on screen. Reminders that come due together take turns: the next shows once the first is acted on. A reminder that comes due while another app is in front, or the TV is off, is skipped and dropped, as the brief says for a Plasma without "Display over other apps"; the alarm and the overlay over other apps come with that permission (M5). Switching automatically at the start isn't offered yet; the brief has it off by default. Deleting a source deletes its reminders. | Brief 5.7. |
| Search | Programs are found in a full-text index of the guide's titles and subtitles (SQLite FTS4, accents ignored): each word typed matches the start of a word, so "night st" finds "Endurance 24: Night Stint", but "night" doesn't find "Midnight". Channel names match anywhere and bring up what's on the channel now. Results update 150 ms after the last key: up to 50, title matches first, then subtitle matches, then the words in another order; within each, what's on now first. Hidden and locked channels, programs that are over on a channel without an archive, and channels without a guide don't show; the index leaves them out before it picks the nearest. Movies and Series come from the sample world until VOD (M4). | Brief 4.3 and 5.10. The index can't find the middle of a word; scanning every program for it would be slow on a TV with a week's guide. |
| Search results | OK on a program on now plays its channel. OK on one coming up opens Remind Me and Record, and its card says "Reminder set". Catch-up results play the channel live until catch-up playback is in. Opening any result keeps the search among the last 10, shown under Suggestions. | Brief 4.3 and 5.10. |
| Catch-up addresses | Xtream's timeshift address, with the start written in the server's own time zone and the program's length from the guide (an hour when the guide doesn't know it). Playlists' catch-up templates (default, append, shift, Flussonic, xc, vod) as IPTV Simple and TiviMate read them, with times in UTC; a playlist channel with catch-up days but no mode uses `xc` beside an Xtream-style address, otherwise `shift`. A playlist whose server reads `xc` times in its own time zone needs the catch-up time offset that comes with per-source settings (M3). Archives go straight to VLC rather than through the stream hub. | Brief 5.5, 7.2 and 7.3. An archive is a recording: the hub's reconnects are for live streams. |
| Playing catch-up | OK on a past program with ↺ in the Guide, a catch-up result in Search, or a program on the Catch-up screen plays it from its start. Start Over (the button, Player Options' Watch from the Start, or green) plays the program on now from its start. The overlay shows the program from the archive, marked Catch-up instead of LIVE; Go Live returns to live TV, and when the program ends, or the archive catches up with live TV, live TV plays. Play/Pause pauses it; after more than a minute, playing opens the archive again where it stopped, in case the server let the connection go. An archive that stops well before its program's end shows a message, and Try Again carries on from there. In the Guide, ↺ goes only as far back as the channel's archive. Since M6, Left and Right on the timeline move within it (the archive opens again there, once a run of presses stops), and coming back to Plasma during catch-up starts the program over. | Brief 4.8, 4.10 and 5.5. |
| Watch from the Start hint | After a zap or a typed number, on a channel with an archive, when the program started at least 5 minutes ago and has at least 10 left: "Started 1 h 41 min ago · OK to watch from the start" in the zap banner, which stays up for 8 s. OK opens the overlay on Start Over, so a second OK starts it; green or ◀◀ start over at once. Once per program; Settings › Playback › Offer Watch from the Start turns it off. | Brief 4.11. |
| Catch-up screen | Lists the channels whose provider keeps an archive, by number; hidden and locked ones stay out. Each channel's past programs load from the guide as it shows. With no archive anywhere, it says so. | Brief 4.18. |
| Remote Key Test and Learn a Key | Settings › Remote & Buttons › Remote Key Test lists each key pressed with its name, Android's key and scan codes, the remote it came from, and whether Plasma uses it; every key stops there, and Back leaves. Learn a Key teaches Plasma a key: press it, then choose Channel Up, Channel Down, Options, Info, a color or a number, and it acts as that key everywhere. OK on a learned key offers Forget. The D-pad, OK, Back, Home, volume and power keys stay as they are. | The owner's remote: its extra keys may not reach apps on Google TV (22 October). |
| Guides with repeats | A guide that lists a program twice keeps one, and with several playlist guides, a guide channel's programs come from the first guide that has them. | Screens list programs by channel and start. |
| Database version 2 | Adds reminders, the search index and recent searches. A TV with M1's database moves to it with Room's automatic migration, which puts the guide already there into the index; a test checks it against M1's schema. | M1's builds may already be installed. |

## Milestone 3: sources, failover and the backup engine

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A source's actions | OK on a source in Portals & Playlists offers Edit, Refresh Now, Set as Primary (not on the primary source), Test Connection, Account Info, Playback for This Source, Disable or Enable, and Delete Source, in the brief's order. | Brief 4.20. |
| Edit | Opens Add a Source filled in with the source's details, its kind fixed: "‹ Portals & Playlists · Edit Backup Line". The password isn't shown; left empty, the stored one stays. Save checks the new details and loads the channels again; if that fails, the source stays exactly as it was, and the message says why. The source keeps its id, so favorites, numbers, history and reminders stay. | Brief 4.20. |
| Set as Primary | The source moves to the top, the others keep their order behind it. Channel numbers stay put until the next refresh numbers them; the primary source's channels keep a number two sources both want from then on. The Primary source switch on Add a Source does the same for a new source. | Brief 5.1 and 5.2: numbers don't jump on their own. |
| Test Connection | Signs in to an Xtream server again (which also updates the account's details), or asks a playlist's server for the playlist, and times it. While it runs the source says "Testing…"; then "Connected · 42 ms" in its detail, or "Can't connect" with the reason. Refresh Now clears the result. | Brief 4.20. |

## Milestone 6: recording, rewind and local catch-up

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rewind Buffer | Settings › Playback › Rewind and Local Catch-up offers the board's choices: Off, When I pause · 1 hour or 2 hours, Always · 1, 2 or 4 hours. The default is When I pause · 1 hour, as brief 5.5 has it, though board 31 shows a user who chose Always · 2 hours (brief 9.2, item 5, still open: say if Always should be the default when a USB drive is there). It goes in Plasma's own storage on the USB drive when Save To says so and one is there, otherwise in the TV's cache, and takes no more than the Storage Limit or half the room left. A buffer left by a Plasma that stopped suddenly is deleted at the next start. | Brief 5.5 and 4.31. |
| How it's kept | In the stream hub, so every player gets the same: files of about 2 seconds, cut at the video's keyframes (H.264, HEVC, MPEG-2, or the broadcaster's random-access mark), each with the time it came in; a stream without keyframes it can read is cut every 4 seconds at a packet. With When I pause, nothing is written until a pause or a rewind; then what memory holds goes first, with the times it came in. Changing channel ends it and frees the space. When storage runs out or the USB drive goes, what's kept stays and nothing more is written. | Brief 5.5. |
| Pausing live TV | Play/Pause pauses the picture; the hub keeps reading into the buffer, and Play carries on from the byte the player stopped at, without reopening. Paused for about as long as the buffer keeps, or longer, Play opens it 10 seconds after the oldest moment it still holds, so it isn't let go of as it plays; so does a rewind to the oldest moments of a full buffer. The overlay's timeline says how far behind live the picture is, counting up while paused. With the buffer Off, a channel with an archive holds its picture, and Play opens the archive from that moment; without an archive, Play/Pause does nothing on live TV. | Brief 5.5. |
| Rewinding and Go Live | Left and Right on the timeline move 10 seconds; once a run of presses rests for 0.4 s, the player opens the buffer at the keyframe at or before that moment. Further back than the buffer goes, a channel with an archive plays the archive, and one without goes to the oldest moment held; within 3 seconds of live is live. Before a pause starts the buffer, the timeline reaches back as far as memory holds (about 8 seconds at 8 Mb/s), and rewinding starts the buffer. Go Live opens the stream again at the live edge. Switching player starts again at the live edge. | Brief 5.5. |
| Seeking without a local playlist | The hub serves the buffer from a byte position (`?at=`), from a keyframe, with the stream's PAT and PMT first, and the player opens that, rather than a local HLS playlist of the segments as brief 5.5 describes. Either way a seek reopens the player at the keyframe; this needs no playlist writer and no player's HLS quirks. The segments' times and keyframes are kept as an HLS playlist needs them, for local catch-up and chase play to build on. | Fewer moving parts for the same result. |
| Storage on the Rewind page | The drive the buffer goes to, its room, and what the rewind buffer takes now are real. Recordings and local catch-up read nothing, and Local Catch-up Channels None, until they come later in M6. | |
| Recording a programme | Record on a programme coming up or on now (Remind Me and Record in the Guide, on Live TV and in Search) records it once; Cancel Recording takes away one coming up, and stops one being written, keeping what it has. A recording starts Start Early before the programme (a minute) and ends End Late after it (five minutes), and sport 15 minutes later still (Extra Time for Sports), as Settings › Recording has them when it's set. The guide's category says what it is: a film, sport or news, or else a series when it has an episode number. | Brief 5.6. Record Series and conflicts come next in M6. |
| Starting on time | One exact alarm, for the next recording to start, 30 seconds before its padding begins (Plasma declares `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, and `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` up to Android 12); a window alarm opening a minute early where exact ones aren't allowed. It starts a foreground service of the `specialUse` type, which isn't capped at six hours a day; a partial wake lock and a Wi-Fi lock keep the stream coming while the TV is off. After a restart, the alarm is set again, and a recording that should be running carries on, in the same file. | Brief 5.6. |
| Writing | The service opens the channel through the stream hub, so a channel someone's watching shares its connection, and writes the MPEG-TS as it comes to "Channel – Title – 2026-10-21 1945.ts" with a JSON of what the guide said, and a keyframe index (a byte offset and time about every 2 s) for chase play later. A merged channel's sources are tried in order. When the hub gives up on the stream, the service opens the channel again every 10 seconds until the end, adding to the same file, and the tile says Incomplete; when nothing could be recorded, it says Couldn't record and leaves no file. A channel the hub can't hold, HLS among them, can't be recorded yet. | Brief 5.6. |
| Where recordings go | Record To: Plasma's own storage on the USB drive when one is connected (the default), otherwise on the TV (`Android/data/<app>/files/Recordings`). Network folder records to the drive or the TV until network folders come, later in M6; Copy to Network Folder reads Off. | Brief 5.6 and 4.34. |
| Recordings | Board 17 on what's recorded and coming up: the grid newest first, a series' episodes in a folder by title, the one being written marked REC, Coming Up with the next three, Scheduled with the reminders, and the drive's room in Storage. OK plays a recording from its file in the movie player, from where it was left; watched from 92 %. One still being written plays from its start to where it had got when it opened, until chase play comes. Delete removes the files; Keep is kept for when retention comes. The header counts the connections recordings use when the primary source has a limit. The screensaver's line says what's recording. | Brief 4.17 and 5.6. |
| Record Series | Record Series in Remind Me and Record (the Guide, Live TV and Search), and Record Every… in Player Options for an episode, keeps a rule for the title on that channel. It sets every airing in the guide's next eight days, as rules and the guide change and each hour: an episode already recorded or set isn't set again (the same season and episode, else the same episode title, else the same start). An airing cancelled, or skipped in a conflict, isn't set again, but a repeat of it is. Cancel Series, or Delete Rule in Recordings › Series Rules, takes the rule away with what it set that's still to come; what it recorded stays. A rule's recordings are a series in Recordings, in a folder, with two red dots in Coming Up. | Brief 4.8, 4.10 and 5.6. |
| Conflicts | Recordings coming up or being written that overlap with more of them than their source's connection limit allows at once are a conflict: Coming Up's header counts them in amber, and each card in one says Conflict. OK on it opens "You can record 2 things at once. Choose what to skip.", which cancels the one chosen. A source without a limit has none. Watching counts too when it happens: a recording that finds every connection in use can't record, and says so. | Brief 5.6 and 4.35. |
| Retention | A rule's Keep Last (all, unless set) lets its oldest recordings go once a newer one is recorded, after Keep Last was chosen, so lowering it deletes nothing straight away; Settings › Recording › Delete Watched Recordings (Never, unless set) lets watched ones go that long after they were watched, and no sooner than that long after it was chosen. Kept recordings (green) never go. A recording that will go within two days says so in amber, with the rule's Keep Last. When Space Runs Low (Delete oldest watched, unless set): below 2 GB free, the oldest watched recordings on that drive go until there's 4 GB, or with Stop Recording, the recording stops there. Retention runs as recordings finish or are watched, and each hour. | Brief 5.6. A rule's options in Recordings › Series Rules change its Keep Last: all, then the newest 3, 5 or 10. |
| Player Options while recording | Record Program records the programme on now; Stop Recording stops one being written, keeping what it has. | Brief 4.10. |
