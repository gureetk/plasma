# Decisions

What's been decided since the brief (`docs/DESIGN.md`), and choices made while building. These take precedence over the matching open items in the brief's section 9.2.

## Milestone 1: live TV

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sample world or live | Until the first source is added, Plasma runs on the boards' sample world, with the clock at 20:41 on Wed 21 Oct. From then on it runs on the source's channels and the real time; deleting the last source brings the sample world back. Catch-up's sample programs, Recordings, Multiview and Who's Watching stay on sample data until their milestones; Search, Movies, Series and Home's Continue Watching and New in Movies have moved to the sources' data in M2 and M4, and Home's Apps row and Apps to the TV's own apps in M5, in the sample world too. | Every screen has something to show from the first start. |
| Wiring | Plain constructors in one `AppGraph`, built when the app starts, instead of a dependency-injection library. | Few enough parts that a library adds more than it saves. |
| Sources in M1 | Xtream Codes logins and M3U playlists by address. Stalker portals come with M3; Connect on one says so. | The server tested with is Xtream, and it also hands out its list as an M3U playlist. |
| Keeping a source | A new source whose channels don't load isn't kept, so Connect can simply be tried again, even when Add a Source is left while it connects. A refresh that fails, or that finds no live channels at all, keeps the channels and guide there were and notes the problem on the source. A guide refresh is written beside the old guide and shown only once it's complete, in one step. Imports run off the main thread. | A failed import never empties a list (brief 5.1). |
| A source's actions | OK on a source in Portals & Playlists offers Refresh Now and Delete Source; Delete asks first, on Cancel. Edit, Set as Primary, Test Connection, Account Info, Playback for This Source and Disable come with M3. | Enough to try a source and take it away again. |
| Secrets at rest | Xtream passwords, playlist addresses and guide addresses are sealed with a key the Android Keystore holds. Channel addresses from a playlist, which usually carry a login, are encrypted with a data key that the Keystore key seals in turn, because sealing thousands of them through the Keystore would take minutes. | Brief 6.10. |
| Playlist channels | Each keeps its place across refreshes by the stream id at the end of an Xtream-style address (`…/user/pass/1234.ts`), or else by its guide id and name, or else its name; a repeat gets "#2", "#3"… Only live entries are imported: addresses under `/movie/` or `/series/`, or ending in a video file's extension, stay out of Live TV. `#EXTVLCOPT` lines may set the user agent and referrer, nothing else. | Addresses often carry a token that changes (brief 7.3). |
| Playlist guides | The guide addresses typed in on Add a Source, or else the ones the playlist names (`url-tvg`, `x-tvg-url`). With several guides, a channel takes its programs from the first that has it. | |
| How streams play | Live MPEG-TS over http(s) goes through the stream hub; an address ending in `.m3u8`, and `udp://`, `rtmp://` and the like, go straight to VLC. When the hub finds an HLS or DASH playlist behind an address that didn't say so, or can't open an address at all, VLC opens it from the provider itself. A live stream that ends is reported as the source stopping, with Try Again. | Brief 5.4.2. |
| The stream hub's address | The hub listens on 127.0.0.1 itself, the address it gives the engines. Java's own loopback address is `::1` on Android, which 127.0.0.1 doesn't reach: on the Chromecast tested with, no channel played, with any engine, before this was found. | Found on the TV, 28 October. |
| Leaving Plasma and the TV turning off | The picture and sound stop at once; the provider connection stays open for a minute, so coming straight back plays on from the live edge without reconnecting, and then it's let go. Right after a zap, the connection still open is the channel before's, so it goes at once. When the TV Turns Off: Stop at Once lets it go straight away, Keep Playing doesn't stop at all. A partial wake lock (70 s) keeps the minute running. On a box (no speakers of its own), the HDMI output going away counts as the TV turning off. | Brief 5.20. A TV's own Android sees its soundbar's HDMI come and go, so the HDMI hint is for boxes only. |
| When the TV turns on | Last Channel opens the player on the last channel watched (unless the player is already showing), Plasma Home goes to Home, TV Guide to the Guide. Coming back from another app carries on where Plasma was. Since M5 it applies when Plasma comes up within a minute of the TV coming on (time for a profile picker or the launcher first), and when the TV starts with Plasma as its Home or with the override on; Plasma opened later, from another app, carries on. | Brief 5.13. |
| Match Frame Rate | The full-screen player only, never Home's muted picture. 25 and 29.97 fps ask for 50 and 59.94, the rate deinterlaced broadcasts play at. Android 12 and up honor "seamless only"; Android 11 takes the rate as a hint; older TVs switch the display mode only with "always", since that blanks the screen. | Brief 5.4.4. |
| Audio passthrough | libVLC passes through each format the HDMI output reports and decodes the rest, so Passthrough and Auto behave alike with VLC; Decode to PCM turns passthrough off. Settings › Audio shows the output and its formats as this TV reports them. | Brief 5.4.6; per-format choice is VLC's own. |
| APKs | One APK per ABI (`armeabi-v7a`, `arm64-v8a`) plus a universal one with both. libVLC's x86 libraries stay out. With VLC alone, Plasma Dev is 48 MB for `armeabi-v7a`, 63 MB for `arm64-v8a` and 104 MB universal. | Brief 5.18 and 6.8, and the test setup; the sizes are the M1 measurement the brief asks for (6.7). |
| Locked channels | Opening a locked channel asks for the PIN: since M7 the one chosen in Settings › Parental Controls (see Milestone 7), and 1234 in the sample world. Until it's unlocked, a locked channel shows no live picture in Live TV's and the Guide's previews or behind Home, and zapping onto it in the player asks for the PIN. | |
| Screen on while playing | The screen is kept on while live video opens or plays, so the TV's own screensaver doesn't stop Plasma and the channel with it. Plasma's own screensaver still comes after ten idle minutes away from the player. | Brief 5.14 and 5.20. |
| Opening and buffering | While a channel, a movie or an episode opens, or fills its buffer again, a ring turns in the middle of the player. It waits 0.4 s first, so a quick start never flashes it. Since 11 November a smaller ring (48 px) turns the same way in the middle of Live TV's and the Guide's pictures while their channel opens, and goes when it plays, fails or ends; until its picture comes, the picture's background fills the slot to its rounded corners, where the last channel's picture had shown. | No board draws it; nothing showed that a movie was starting, nor in a picture that a channel was. Brief 2.9, honest status. |
| Still to come in the player | Pause and rewind act on live TV since M6's rewind buffer (see Milestone 6), and Start Over since M2. In Player Options, Switch Player works since M3, and so do Audio and Subtitles, Picture Size, the stream info and Sleep Timer (see Milestone 3). | |

## Milestone 2: the guide, catch-up, reminders and search

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Guide by day | Each day tab loads that day's programs from the database, with four hours either side of midnight, and shows empty cells while they load. The Group button (or yellow) picks All Channels, Favorites or one of the provider's groups; Favorites count from 1 here too. A channel number jumps to its row at the same time. | Brief 4.8. |
| An out-of-date guide | When no guide has been loaded for two days, or the programs run out within 12 hours, an amber line under the Guide's header says when it was last updated, with Refresh. | Brief 5.3. |
| Reminders | Kept in the database per program, so they survive a restart, and shown as bells in the Guide and on Live TV. A minute before the start, the toast from board 11 comes up with Switch Now focused and stays until it's acted on or the program ends. In the player it's part of the player, so channel and number keys still work under it; over any other screen it's a window of its own, so the screen keeps its focus and gets it back. Since M5 it can show over other apps too (see Milestone 5). | Brief 4.11 and 5.7. |
| When reminders fire | From Plasma's own clock while Plasma is on screen. Reminders that come due together take turns: the next shows once the first is acted on. A reminder that comes due while another app is in front, or the TV is off, is skipped and dropped, as the brief says for a Plasma without "Display over other apps"; the alarm and the overlay over other apps come with that permission (M5). Switching automatically at the start came with #115 (see "Small items", 4 November 2026), off by default as the brief has it. Deleting a source deletes its reminders. | Brief 5.7. |
| Search | Programs are found in a full-text index of the guide's titles and subtitles (SQLite FTS4, accents ignored): each word typed matches the start of a word, so "night st" finds "Endurance 24: Night Stint", but "night" doesn't find "Midnight". Channel names match anywhere and bring up what's on the channel now; since 28 October the channels themselves come first, in a Channels section. Results update 150 ms after the last key: up to 50, title matches first, then subtitle matches, then the words in another order; within each, what's on now first. Hidden and locked channels, programs that are over on a channel without an archive, and channels without a guide don't show; the index leaves them out before it picks the nearest. Movies and Series come from the sample world until VOD (M4). | Brief 4.3 and 5.10. The index can't find the middle of a word; scanning every program for it would be slow on a TV with a week's guide. |
| Search results | OK on a program on now plays its channel. OK on one coming up opens Remind Me and Record, and its card says "Reminder set". Catch-up results play the channel live until catch-up playback is in. Opening any result keeps the search among the last 10, shown under Suggestions. | Brief 4.3 and 5.10. |
| Catch-up addresses | Xtream's timeshift address, with the start written in the server's own time zone and the program's length from the guide (an hour when the guide doesn't know it). Playlists' catch-up templates (default, append, shift, Flussonic, xc, vod) as IPTV Simple and TiviMate read them, with times in UTC; a playlist channel with catch-up days but no mode uses `xc` beside an Xtream-style address, otherwise `shift`. A playlist whose server reads `xc` times in its own time zone needs the catch-up time offset that comes with per-source settings (M3). Archives go straight to VLC rather than through the stream hub. | Brief 5.5, 7.2 and 7.3. An archive is a recording: the hub's reconnects are for live streams. |
| Playing catch-up | OK on a past program with ↺ in the Guide, a catch-up result in Search, or a program on the Catch-up screen plays it from its start. Start Over (the button, Player Options' Watch from the Start, or green) plays the program on now from its start. The overlay shows the program from the archive, marked Catch-up instead of LIVE; Go Live returns to live TV, and when the program ends, or the archive catches up with live TV, live TV plays. Play/Pause pauses it; after more than a minute, playing opens the archive again where it stopped, in case the server let the connection go. An archive that stops well before its program's end shows a message, and Try Again carries on from there. In the Guide, ↺ goes only as far back as the channel's archive. Since M6, Left and Right on the timeline move within it (the archive opens again there, once a run of presses stops). Coming back to Plasma during catch-up started the program over until #117; now it carries on where it was (see "Small items", 4 November 2026). | Brief 4.8, 4.10 and 5.5. |
| Watch from the Start hint | After a zap or a typed number, on a channel with an archive, when the program started at least 5 minutes ago and has at least 10 left: "Started 1 h 41 min ago · OK to watch from the start" in the zap banner, which stays up for 8 s. OK opens the overlay on Start Over, so a second OK starts it; green or ◀◀ start over at once. Once per program; Settings › Playback › Offer Watch from the Start turns it off. | Brief 4.11. |
| Catch-up screen | Lists the channels whose provider keeps an archive, by number; hidden and locked ones stay out. Each channel's past programs load from the guide as it shows. With no archive anywhere, it says so. | Brief 4.18. |
| Remote Key Test and Learn a Key | Settings › Remote & Buttons › Remote Key Test lists each key pressed with its name, Android's key and scan codes, the remote it came from, and whether Plasma uses it; every key stops there, and Back leaves. Learn a Key teaches Plasma a key: press it, then choose Channel Up, Channel Down, Options, Info, a color or a number, and it acts as that key everywhere. OK on a learned key offers Forget. ## Milestone 6: recording, rewind and local catch-up

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rewind Buffer | Settings › Playback › Rewind and Local Catch-up offers the board's choices: Off, When I pause · 1 hour or 2 hours, Always · 1, 2 or 4 hours. The default is When I pause · 1 hour, as brief 5.5 has it, though board 31 shows a user who chose Always · 2 hours (brief 9.2, item 5, still open: say if Always should be the default when a USB drive is there). It goes in Plasma's own storage on the USB drive when Save To says so and one is there, otherwise in the TV's cache, and takes no more than the Storage Limit or half the room left. | How it's kept | In the stream hub, so every player gets the same: files of about 2 seconds, cut at the video's keyframes (H.264, HEVC, MPEG-2, or the broadcaster's random-access mark), each with the time it came in; a stream without keyframes it can read is cut every 4 seconds at a packet. With When I pause, nothing is written until a pause or a rewind; then what memory holds goes first, with the times it came in. Changing channel ends it and frees the space. When storage runs out or the USB drive goes, what's kept stays and nothing more is written. | Brief 5.5. |
| Pausing live TV | Play/Pause pauses the picture; the hub keeps reading into the buffer, and Play carries on from the byte the player stopped at, without reopening. The overlay's timeline says how far behind live the picture is, counting up while paused. With the buffer Off, a channel with an archive holds its picture, and Play opens the archive from that moment; without an archive, Play/Pause does nothing on live TV. | Brief 5.5. |
| Rewinding and Go Live | Left and Right on the timeline move 10 seconds; once a run of presses rests for 0.4 s, the player opens the buffer at the keyframe at or before that moment. Before a pause starts the buffer, the timeline reaches back as far as memory holds (about 8 seconds at 8 Mb/s), and rewinding starts the buffer. Go Live opens the stream again at the live edge. Switching player starts again at the live edge. | Brief 5.5. |
| Seeking without a local playlist | The hub serves the buffer from a byte position (`?at=`), from a keyframe, with the stream's PAT and PMT first, and the player opens that, rather than a local HLS playlist of the segments as brief 5.5 describes. The segments' times and keyframes are kept as an HLS playlist needs them, for local catch-up and chase play to build on. | Fewer moving parts for the same result. |
| Storage on the Rewind page | The drive the buffer goes to, its room, and what the rewind buffer, recordings and local catch-up take now are real; so are Local Catch-up Channels and the amber line on the primary source's connections. | Brief 4.31. |

| Writing | The service opens the channel through the stream hub, so a channel someone's watching shares its connection, and writes the MPEG-TS as it comes to "Channel – Title – 2026-10-21 1945.ts" with a JSON of what the guide said, and a keyframe index (a byte offset and time about every 2 s) for chase play later. | Recording HLS | An HLS channel goes straight to the engine, not through the hub (brief 5.4.2), so a recording reads its playlist itself: the variant with the most bandwidth, from three segments behind live, each new segment downloaded in order and added to the file as it comes, AES-128 ones decrypted, the playlist read again every target duration (half that when nothing was new). | Chase play | OK on a recording being written plays it in the movie player as it's written, from where it was left or its start (brief 4.17). ### Choices made while building it

| Topic | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Restart Frozen Streams | Settings › Playback › Restart Frozen Streams: After 5 Seconds (the default), After 10 Seconds or Off. Off turns it off entirely. | What counts as playing | Each engine's own count of what it puts out, looked at once a second: VLC's statistics for the stream (sound buffers played, and pictures decoded less those it lost, as `KeptOutput` counts them; see "Freeze Watch, and frozen streams on VLC" below), read on VLC's own thread; mpv's playback position and sound position (`time-pos` and `audio-pts`), each change counted; the Android player's decoders' counts of frames rendered and sound handed on. A still or black picture is still frames, and a radio channel's sound counts. An engine that can't count goes by its position instead, and nothing counts as frozen until what it goes by has moved, so an engine that never reports movement never restarts anything. | What the viewer sees and hears. VLC's own time follows the stream as it's read, so it can move on through such a freeze. | When it's frozen | The count hasn't moved for the setting's time while a live channel plays at the live edge through the stream hub: the engine playing (not paused, buffering or opening), the hub not reconnecting, after its first picture (a radio channel's first sound), not behind live in the rewind buffer, and counted afresh when a seek or Go Live opens the stream again. | |
| The hub's data | A stream counts as frozen only if more of it came through the hub while its picture and sound stood still. One that stopped arriving too is the hub's to handle: it reconnects after 15 s without data, and fails over to another source. Restarting it sooner would throw away a connection that may come back by itself (a Wi-Fi blip, a busy server), could turn a short drop into an error, and couldn't tell an engine at fault for the repeats. | The case seen has the data arriving. Counting what came while the picture stood still, rather than over the last second or two, also suits servers that send in bursts. |
| The restart | What a zap away and back does, on the same source and engine: the engine stops and the connection goes; the channel's address is asked for again (a portal's expires); a new connection opens, and the engine plays it from the live edge. The channel, its list, the zap history, Recently Watched and the overlay stay as they were, and the sources aren't planned again, so Remember What Works doesn't count it as a tune. As on a zap, a recording of the channel keeps the connection it shares open, and with Rewind Buffer on Always, what the buffer held goes. | Changing channel and straight back fixes it every time. |
| Repeats | The third freeze within 10 minutes on one engine, with the data arriving, hands the stream to the next engine on the same connection, as three stalls do: the fallback notice ("VLC couldn't play Arena Sports 2. Playing it with mpv."), the amber dot, and the engine kept for the channel once it has played for 30 s. The next engine starts with no freezes counted. | The setting's row | In Playback's More section, under Connection Timeout, below the fold, so board 27's first screen stays as drawn. ## Encode Surround to Dolby Digital (#102, 6 November 2026)

Plasma's libmpv build (0.41.0-plasma.1) has FFmpeg's AC-3 encoder and S/PDIF muxer, so this is #102 in Plasma itself: Encode Surround, mpv's passthrough formats and the "passthrough" label.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| When it acts | Settings › Playback › Audio › Encode Surround to Dolby Digital on; sound passing through (Audio Output on Passthrough, or on Auto with a sound system that takes Dolby or DTS; not with Decode to PCM, Stereo Downmix or Decode This Channel); Dolby Digital among the formats passed; and a sound system that takes no more than two channels of decoded sound: over ARC, or an HDMI output that reports two channels or doesn't say. Then sound of 3 or more channels that can't pass through as it is reaches it as Dolby Digital 5.1 at 640 kb/s: AAC 5.1, and Dolby Digital Plus, DTS or TrueHD where those don't pass through. Stereo, and sound that passes through, stay as they are. | Brief 5.4.6. A receiver that takes decoded surround (HDMI or eARC) gets it as PCM, with nothing lost, so nothing is encoded for it. |
| How | mpv's `lavcac3enc` filter (`tospdif=yes:bitrate=640:minch=3`). Live Preview on Home has a budget of its own. |
| Where they run | `./gradlew :benchmark:connectedBenchmarkAndroidTest`, on a device or an emulator connected over adb. By hand, it can instead put the two APKs on the Plasma Benchmarks pre-release, to install on a TV with adb alone; the benchmark build isn't a Home app, so installing it doesn't make the TV ask which Home to use. Plasma Benchmark starts over a black screen of the benchmarks' own, so stopping it doesn't bring back the TV's Home, which a launcher's Home button override opens over. | An emulator on GitHub's machines isn't a TV box, so its numbers show changes from one run to the next; a TV's own come from running them on it (`docs/testing.md`). |
| Where a cold start's time goes | `StartupSplitMetric`, from Android starting to launch Plasma to Plasma's own `Plasma.ready` trace section, opened as it reports full display: that time, and the time to its first live world; its main thread running, waiting for a processor, asleep and waiting on storage; the processor time of all its threads, and of its drawing, database, coroutine, garbage collector and compiler threads; and its own sections for sorting the guide's window, working out the channel lineup and making the live world. A second cold start, `coldStartToHomeCachesKept`, keeps what Android keeps between starts, such as Plasma's compiled shaders, which macrobenchmark's cold start clears first. | So that a cold start over budget says what to fix, on the emulator and on a TV, where there's only the results file to go on. The sections cost nothing while nothing traces. Clearing the caches is the first start after an update; keeping them is a start after Android stopped Plasma in the background. |
| Whether the graphics chip keeps up | `FrameSplitMetric` also gives, a frame at a time, the RenderThread's wait for a buffer to draw into (`frameDequeueMs`), its sending of the drawing to the graphics chip (`frameFlushMs`) and its handing the frame over (`frameSwapMs`), and the chip's own time to finish the frame, as HWUI's GPU completion thread waits for it (`frameGpuMs`), each where the device's trace has it. | A frame's drawing that takes too long is either too much to send, which is the RenderThread's own work, or too much for the chip to draw, which shows as the chip's time and the wait for a buffer: they're fixed differently. |
| Which composables take a frame's time | The benchmark build has Compose's composition tracing (`androidx.compose.runtime:runtime-tracing`, through the BOM). A Benchmarks run by hand with "compose_tracing" turns it on (`androidx.benchmark.perfettoSdkTracing.enable`), so the traces name each composable, and the trace checks print each benchmark's main-thread time by section, its own time without the sections within it. | Brief 6.7's 60 fps: a frame's time says how far off it is, not which part of a screen to fix. It's off by default, since tracing every composable slows what's measured. |
| Movies, Series and Home's rows (11 November) | The made-up source is an Xtream server, with the same 1,500 channels and guide, 6,000 movies in 24 categories and 1,200 series in 12, each with a poster: the sample world's pictures in turn, under an address of its own, as a provider's are. Home's cold start now has those rows, and their posters, to show. | Brief 6.7's 60 fps is for every screen, and its 100 ms from a key to what it changes too. A playlist has no movies or series, and the cold start had nothing of the kind to load. |
| Plasma's trace data only (11 November) | Each benchmark records macrobenchmark's own trace, except that its `track_event` data source, which composition tracing writes to, comes from Plasma alone (`measurePlasma` in `Trace.kt`). Otherwise Perfetto waits up to 30 s for every app that writes trace data of its own, and an app Android has frozen in the background can't start. When that app is woken partway through an iteration, as happens when macrobenchmark runs `dumpsys gfxinfo` to check a launch's first frame, the trace ends there. A cold start's trace can then end before the frame after full display, which the time to full display needs. | No metric uses other apps' trace data, and Plasma's composition tracing still reaches the trace. |

## Cold start (10 November 2026): Home opens on its own channels' guide (#127)

Brief 6.7's budget for a cold start to an interactive Home is 1.5 s. The launch screen went once the live world was in, and that waited for the guide window, from 3 hours back to 15 ahead, for every channel: with 6.7's big source, about 23,000 programs. On the emulator (Benchmarks run 14), the channels were in half a second after the launch, and the live world four seconds later, as soon as the window's query had finished.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Home's first look | As Plasma starts, the live world's first value can be a first look: the world without the guide window, with only the guide of the channels Home shows, read on their own (the channel at its top, the favorites, and the channels Upcoming Events looks at while that row is on). It goes as soon as it's made, unless the whole world is in by then; the whole world follows once the window has loaded, and nothing of the first look comes after it. | Home's rows show the guide of those channels alone, and a few channels' guide reads in milliseconds on their own index. |
| Who sees it | Only Home (`AppGraph.homeWorld`), and the launch screen goes on it when Home is the first screen. Every other screen, and everything else that reads the live world, has the whole window as before (`AppGraph.world`), and the launch screen waits for it when another screen opens first. Live TV or the Guide opened from Home before it's in shows the wave wordmark flowing, as on the launch screen, once the wait has gone on 300 ms (#158). | Live TV, the Guide, the player and the rest show any channel's guide, and Age Limit goes by what's on, so a world with only some channels' guide would show the others empty, and unlocked, for a moment. |
| The query | A few channels' window is its own query (`ProgrammeDao.forKeys`), with `CROSS JOIN` so SQLite reads the programmes first, on the `(epgKey, startAt)` index, whatever it would guess of the join; at most 500 channels a query. | So the plan doesn't depend on the SQLite version on the TV. |
| The launch screen (11 November) | It goes as soon as the first screen has what it shows, whether the word has come together or not, rather than at 0.95 s at the soonest (#158): it's there for as long as there's something to wait for, and no longer. | With the first frame 0.4 to 0.5 s into a cold start, the 0.95 s after it were the least a cold start could take, more than a quick one needed. |
| The player | The player, and libVLC with it, is made off the main thread once the first screen has what it shows (`AppGraph.makePlayer`), rather than as Plasma's root first draws with a source; until then the video layer has the stills. A screen that plays, such as the player or Live TV, still makes it as it opens. `LocalLiveVideo` is a dynamic composition local, so the player arriving composes again only the video layer, not every screen under it. | Making it loads libVLC and asks Android's sound service about its outputs: a few hundred milliseconds of the main thread on the emulator, while Home waited to be drawn. A picture takes longer than that to come anyway. |

## Fewer compositions at each move (11 November 2026, #127)

Brief 6.7's 60 fps: composition tracing in the benchmarks showed every channel row on screen composed again at each move, in Live TV and the Guide, where only the two rows focus moves between change, and a row growing with focus composed again at each frame of its growth.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A row's own focus | A row in Live TV's list or the Guide reads whether focus is on it (and in the Guide, which of its programmes) through `derivedStateOf`, and in Live TV whether its channel is in the picture: a move composes the two rows it's between again. | Each row read the focused channel or the cursor itself, so all of them composed again at each move. |
| Live TV following focus | Live TV follows the focused channel with `snapshotFlow`, for the details that follow focus once it rests and for a Favorites channel kept while focus is on it, rather than as the key of an effect. | An effect's key is read as the screen composes, so the whole screen composed again at each move. |
| The swap fade | `swapFade` draws with one layer block a `SwapFade` keeps, so the modifier it makes is equal to the last. | A new block each time made the modifier differ, so what had it, as the Guide's time header, composed again with its screen. |
| A row growing with focus | Live TV's rows, Settings' rows and a sheet's rows grow with focus through `rowSize`, which reads their animated height and margin as they're laid out. | Read as the row composed, each frame of its 150 ms growth composed it again, with everything in it. |
| Home's fades | Home's top, its hero and the rows above the top one fade through `fade`, which reads the alpha in the layer, as `Modifier.alpha` draws; the backdrop behind the rows comes and goes as the top's fade starts and ends. | Read as Home composed, each frame of a 200 ms move between the top and the rows composed all of Home again. |
| The graphite fill | The dock tiles' fill reads its colors as it draws. | Read as it composed, each frame of its 120 ms brightening composed it again. |
| A number column's width | `textWidth` keeps what it has measured for each of Android's font resolvers, so the rows of a list measure their number column's sample once between them. | Each row measured it as it came on screen and as it took focus, a text layout each time. |
| Movies, Series and Recordings' grids | A poster (or a recording) reads whether it's where focus goes into its grid through `derivedStateOf`, and the grid reads where it starts without composing the screen again. | Each poster had the grid's start, worked out from the focused title, so a move composed every poster on screen again, with the screen around them. |
| Home's backdrop behind the rows | `RowsBackdrop` reads the focused item's art itself. | Read as Home composed, each move along a row composed all of Home again. |

## Less drawn at each frame (11 November 2026, #127)

Brief 6.7's 60 fps: at each frame of a move, Android draws all of a screen again, the parts that stay still as well as the part that moves. Nothing here looks different.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Layers kept while still | The top bar, the key hints, a list of categories, what's on under Live TV's picture, the header over Movies' and Series' posters, and the Guide's selected program, days and times are drawn into a layer the GPU keeps (`keptWhileStill`) once focus has been out of them for 0.4 s. A frame then draws each of them as one picture, until something in it changes. While focus is in one, it draws as before. | A kept layer ends at its edges, and focus lifts things past them, with their shadows; 0.4 s lets the lift settle after focus leaves. Nothing kept moves by itself, which would draw the layer again at each frame. |
| A faded row | A list's row faded at a cut-off edge is drawn through a kept layer (`keptAlpha`), and so is a Guide program faded as past. | `Modifier.alpha` over a part that overlaps itself is drawn into a new layer at each frame. |
| Rows kept while still | A row of words in a list (Live TV's channels, the categories, Settings' rows and a sheet's) is drawn into a kept layer while it's at its own size, without focus's lift (`PlasmaSurface(keptWhileStill = true)`); a Guide row is while the cursor is in another row and its programs are in (`keptLayer`). | A move changes the two rows it's between, and the others on screen were drawn again at each frame. A layer is drawn at the size it's laid out and ends at its edges, so a row growing with focus, or lifted with its shadow, draws as before. |
| The picture's corners | Over live video, the corners outside the picture's rounded slot are the stroke of a rounded rectangle whose inner edge is the slot's, over the screen's own background past its straight edges. | The GPU draws that without a path, where the path before took it a stencil pass at each frame. |
| A row growing with focus | The parts of Live TV's rows, Settings' rows and a sheet's rows are measured at their own height (`steadyHeight`), whatever height the row has as it grows, and sit where they did. | Measured in the row's height, they were measured again at each frame of its 150 ms growth, and text cut short with "…" laid out again. |
| The turning ring | It reads its angle as it draws. | Read as it composed, it composed again at each frame. |

## Blurred pictures kept as layers (12 November 2026, #127)

Brief 6.7's 60 fps: a blurred picture is among the costliest things Android draws, and Home's rows have one behind them that changes at each move. Nothing here looks different.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A blurred picture | The picture behind Home's rows, Home's hero art from a poster, the enlargement behind a poster in Continue Watching, and a movie's or series' page's backdrop from a poster are each blurred once, into a layer of its own that the GPU keeps (`BlurredImage`). A frame draws that layer as one picture until the picture changes. | Android blurs a blurred layer as it draws it: again at each frame that draws it, unless the graphics driver is one it keeps the result for. A blur as wide as these takes several passes over the picture. |
| One picture fading into the next | Behind Home's rows and in its hero, each picture is drawn at its own alpha as one fades into the next (`FadeBetween`): the kept layer's for a blurred picture, the picture's own for one that isn't. The time and the curve are `Crossfade`'s, as before. | `Crossfade` puts each picture in a faded layer of its own: one more offscreen picture each, drawn again whenever what's in it changes. |
| The picture behind Home's rows at 45 % | Its 45 % is the picture's own alpha, under the blur as before, rather than a faded layer around it. | A faded layer around one picture is an offscreen picture the size of it, for nothing: fading the one thing drawn is the same. |
| A picture coming in | A blurred picture's 200 ms fade as it arrives is drawn on its kept layer, not by the painter under the blur: the app's resolver gives the painter without its fade, and how far the picture has come in (`ArrivingImageResolver`). One already in memory still shows at once. | Under the blur, each frame of the fade changed what's in the layer, so the picture was blurred again at each. A blur of a faded picture is that fade of the blurred picture, so it looks the same. |
| Only while it shows | A blurred picture that isn't there yet, or has faded out behind the one after it, is neither blurred nor kept (its layers are on only while its alpha is above 0). | Android gives a layer its memory whether or not it's seen, and moving along a row quickly, with the key held, leaves a picture for each poster passed on its way out. |
| The reference images | Home's rows' four images are recorded again: one step in 255 lighter on about a tenth of their pixels, in the picture behind the top of the rows. Every other image is the same, the other blurred pictures' included. | The 45 % is applied to the picture as it's drawn, where it was applied to a copy of it, and the two round differently. |
| Not changed | The player's still picture behind a sheet (`Still` in `VideoLayer.kt`), which only the sample data has; and the screensaver's pictures, which fade into the next once in a few minutes, with nothing else moving. | Neither is drawn while a move waits on a frame. |

## Blurred pictures from a smaller copy (12 November 2026, #127)

Brief 6.7's 60 fps. A blur as wide as Home's leaves only broad patches of color, and a much smaller copy of the picture has those too.

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A picture under a wide blur | It's decoded, blurred and kept as a smaller copy of itself, and the kept layer is stretched back over its place as it's drawn. The copy is a quarter the size each way under CSS `blur(60px)` (the picture behind Home's rows, Home's hero art from a poster, a movie's or series' page's backdrop from a poster) and half the size under `blur(28px)` (the enlargement behind a poster in Continue Watching), and its own blur is as wide for it as the whole picture's was. | A copy a quarter the size each way is a sixteenth of the pixels to decode, to blur and to keep in the GPU's memory, for every picture, and a row moved along brings a new picture at each move. |
| How close it is | It can't be told from the whole picture blurred, and it isn't the same to the last step. `BackdropsTest` holds the two within 4 steps in 255 of each other away from the picture's edges (3 at Home's rows' 45 %) and within 6 at its edges, where a blur carries the outermost pixels outward and the copy's are its own; the seven reference images that have a blurred picture are recorded again (Home's rows' five, and a movie's and a series' page), each within 2 steps of what it was but for the 65 px along the top edge of the series page's, which are within 5. | It changes how the picture is made, to be kept only if it isn't seen. |
| A narrower blur | A blur with a radius under 40 px (about CSS `blur(23px)`) takes the whole picture, as before. | The copy's own blur has to stay wide enough that stretching it back shows nothing. |
| Where there's no picture | The placeholder's color is drawn as it is, in no layer. | A blur leaves a plain color as it is. |
